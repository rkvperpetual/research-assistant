# NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) — Summary

## Overview

The NIST Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (NIST AI RMF 1.0), published by the National Institute of Standards and Technology in January 2023, is a voluntary framework designed to help organizations manage risks associated with AI systems throughout their lifecycle.

Unlike the EU AI Act, the NIST AI RMF is **not legally binding** — it is guidance-oriented and designed to be adapted to specific organizational contexts, sectors, and use cases.

## Core Structure: Four Functions

The AI RMF is organized around four core functions:

### 1. GOVERN
Establishes organizational practices for AI risk management:
- Policies and culture around AI risk
- Accountability and responsibility structures
- Workforce training and awareness
- Engagement with stakeholders and affected communities

### 2. MAP
Contextualizes AI risk:
- Categorize AI systems by purpose, context, and potential impact
- Identify affected individuals and groups
- Understand the system's deployment context
- Assess potential harms: technical, societal, economic

### 3. MEASURE
Analyzes and assesses AI risk:
- Define metrics for trustworthy AI characteristics
- Test, evaluate, and benchmark AI systems
- Assess explainability, fairness, privacy, robustness, safety, security
- Document assumptions and limitations

### 4. MANAGE
Prioritizes and addresses AI risk:
- Implement risk treatments and mitigations
- Monitor systems post-deployment
- Incident response and recovery
- Communicate risk information to relevant stakeholders

## AI Trustworthiness Characteristics

NIST defines seven characteristics of trustworthy AI:
1. **Accountable and Transparent** — clear documentation and explainability
2. **Explainable and Interpretable** — decisions can be understood by stakeholders
3. **Fair** — free from inappropriate bias; equitable outcomes
4. **Privacy-Enhanced** — protects individual privacy
5. **Reliable and Robust** — performs consistently across conditions
6. **Safe** — does not cause undue harm
7. **Secure and Resilient** — withstands adversarial conditions

## AI RMF Profiles

Organizations create "Profiles" by selecting relevant Framework categories and subcategories based on their risk tolerance, sector requirements, and organizational priorities. Profiles can represent a current state or a target state.

## Comparison to EU AI Act

| Dimension | EU AI Act | NIST AI RMF |
|---|---|---|
| Legal binding | Yes (EU law) | No (voluntary guidance) |
| Risk approach | Prescriptive 4-tier classification | Flexible, context-dependent |
| Enforcement | EU AI Office, national authorities | Self-governance |
| Primary audience | Providers/deployers in EU market | Any organization, global |
| Scope | Product/system regulation | Organizational risk management |
| Specificity | High (explicit prohibited/high-risk lists) | Low (principles and practices) |

The EU AI Act is considerably more **prescriptive** — it mandates specific requirements for specific system types with defined penalties. The NIST AI RMF is more **process-oriented**, providing a flexible structure for any organization to build its own AI risk management practices.

## NIST AI RMF Playbook

NIST also publishes the AI RMF Playbook, which provides actionable guidance, suggested actions, and informative references mapped to each Framework subcategory.
