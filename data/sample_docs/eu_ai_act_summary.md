# EU AI Act — Summary and Risk Classification

## Overview

The EU Artificial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act), officially adopted in 2024, is the world's first comprehensive legal framework regulating artificial intelligence. It applies to providers, deployers, importers, and distributors of AI systems in the EU market.

## Risk-Based Classification (Four-Tier Pyramid)

The Act classifies AI systems into four risk categories:

### 1. Unacceptable Risk (Prohibited)
AI systems that pose a clear threat to fundamental rights are prohibited outright. Examples include:
- Social scoring by public authorities
- Real-time biometric identification in public spaces (with narrow law enforcement exceptions)
- AI systems that exploit psychological vulnerabilities
- AI that manipulates behavior subliminally

### 2. High Risk (Regulated)
High-risk AI systems are permitted but subject to strict requirements (Article 6). They must undergo conformity assessments, maintain technical documentation, and register in an EU database.

High-risk categories include:
- AI used in critical infrastructure (energy, water, transport)
- Educational tools determining access or evaluation outcomes
- Employment and HR systems (e.g., resume screening, performance monitoring)
- Essential private and public services (credit scoring, insurance)
- Law enforcement and border control
- Administration of justice

**Requirements for High-Risk AI:**
- Risk management system
- Data governance
- Technical documentation
- Record-keeping and logging
- Transparency and provision of information to users
- Human oversight
- Robustness, accuracy, and cybersecurity

### 3. Limited Risk (Transparency Obligations)
AI systems with specific transparency obligations. For example:
- Chatbots must inform users they are interacting with AI
- Deepfakes must be labeled as AI-generated

### 4. Minimal/No Risk (Unregulated)
The vast majority of AI applications pose minimal risk (spam filters, AI in video games) and are unregulated under the Act.

## General Purpose AI (GPAI) Models

The Act introduces specific rules for General Purpose AI models:
- All GPAI models must provide technical documentation and comply with copyright law
- GPAI models with systemic risk (training compute > 10^25 FLOPs) face additional obligations: adversarial testing, incident reporting to the EU AI Office, cybersecurity measures

## Governance Structure

- **EU AI Office:** Central enforcement and coordination body
- **National Competent Authorities:** Per-member-state enforcement
- **Scientific Panel:** Independent experts advising on GPAI models

## Penalties

- Prohibited AI violations: up to €35 million or 7% of global turnover
- High-risk violations: up to €15 million or 3% of global turnover
- Incorrect information to authorities: up to €7.5 million or 1.5% of global turnover

## Timeline

- 2024: Act enters into force
- 2025: Prohibited AI rules apply; GPAI rules apply
- 2026: High-risk AI rules fully applicable
- 2027: Some sector-specific rules apply
